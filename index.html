ThreadKeep · Working Session

Two decisions to walk out with

Mike & Chris  ·  June 15, 2026

The build is in good shape — the hard technical work is behind us. What's standing between ThreadKeep and a launch now isn't code; it's two calls we keep deferring. Today is about making them.

Where we are

The pieces that were genuinely risky are done and verified: PDF exports come out accurate and in order, the app only ever reads a copy of your Messages data (never the original), five export formats ship, the Apple developer account is live, and the privacy docs are drafted and ready for a look. The plan still shows ~7% of tasks complete, but that number is misleading — most of what's left is sequenced behind the two decisions below.

Cleared:  PDF accuracy · read-only data access · 5 export formats · Apple Developer + TestFlight · privacy docs drafted

And we're live on the web. threadkeep.xyz is up now — full marketing site with feature screenshots, working checkout (card, Apple Pay, PayPal, Venmo), an install guide, specs, and FAQ. Total domain cost: $1, from Porkbun. The storefront and the product story are real and shippable today.

1 What's Chris's role?

This is the decision that unlocks everything else. Three shapes on the table:

Trial — a time-boxed run on the operations lane, revisit at a checkpoint.  ·  Ongoing — committed launch partner who owns the lane through release.  ·  Advisory — lighter touch, and the ops work stays with Mike.

Four things wait on this answer: the legal package (privacy policy, EULA, disclaimers), owning the tracker, the Apple review prep, and the tester program.

Why now: the legal quotes are the longest lead time in the whole plan and can't start until this is settled. It's been open two weeks — every week it stays open pushes launch out by a week.

2 What do we charge?

Current thinking is $29–49, one-time — not a subscription. The earlier $8.99 idea got cut: at that price the app reads as a throwaway utility to professionals, and we'd need a thousand-plus sales just to break even. A higher price both signals quality and matches the privacy-and-security investment underneath it.

The snag to settle: both the ROI model below and the live site already say $18.99 — roughly half of where we're leaning. So this isn't theoretical: if we move to $29–49, threadkeep.xyz and the checkout need updating before launch. Either commit to $18.99 as the real number, or decide to reprice and treat the current site as a placeholder. To finalize honestly we still want competitor sales data and one willingness-to-pay conversation with a professional user.

The case, in three numbers

Three funding levels, each over five years. Costs are real — straight from the conservative budget. Revenue is a modeled range, not a promise. The headline: every level pays for itself in year two, and the more we put into legal, security, and marketing, the better the return — because that spend is what opens the professional market.

The single biggest risk on the board is the solo-founder bottleneck — one person can't carry QA, security, legal, marketing, and support at once. That's not an aside; it's the whole reason a partner moves the numbers from "lean" toward "upmarket." This meeting is where we decide whether to.

Walk out with two answers

① Chris's role — trial, ongoing, or advisory.

② The price — lock a number, and reprice the ROI to match (or keep $18.99 as the stated floor).
